This section provides a high-level overview of the basic mechanics, which should be more than enough for players to play the game. 

Throughout the game, the Director will let you know of any modifiers or changes to these rules, or other additional options that have become available to you.

### Finding Clues
To find a clue, you need simply:
- Get yourself into a scene where relevant information can be gathered
- Have the right Investigative Ability to discover the clue, and
- Tell the Director you're using it.

<br/>

### Spends
The Director may offer you opportunities to spend a number of points from the relevant *Investigative Ability pool* for additional benefits. You may also offer to spend the points without prompting, and propose a benefit to be obtained by doing so.

For attempted actions that pose little risk of failure, but still constitutes some effort on your Agent's part, the Director may ask you spend a number of points from the relevant *General Ability pool*.

<br/>  

### Tests

> [!tip] ** Simple Tests**
> Roll 1d6 vs Difficulty. After rolling the die, you may choose to spend any number of points from the relevant ability pool to increase your final die result.
> 
> You may retry a failed test if the circumstances are now more favourable than your initial attempt, and you must spend at least 1 pool point.


> [!tip] **Contests**
> > [!info] **Player-Facing Contests**
> > Roll 1d6 vs Difficulty, spending points if desired.
> 
> > [!info] **Full Contests**
> > Each participant acts in turn. The first to fail a roll loses.
> 
> > [!info] **Thriller Chases**
> > Pursuers and runners act in rounds. In each round, they reveal their spends simultaneously before rolling. The Lead increases or decreases based on the successes and failures (and margins thereof). The chase ends when the Lead is reduced to 0 or increased to 10.
