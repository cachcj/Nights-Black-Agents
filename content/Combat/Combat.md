Sometimes, you simply have to get your hands dirty. Combat is a specialised sort of contest, usually involving any of the following abilities:
- Hand-to-Hand vs. Hand-to-Hand, Hand-to-Hand vs. Weapons, or Weapons vs. Weapons: the characters are fighting at close quarters.
- Shooting vs Shooting: the characters are apart from one another and trying to hit each other with guns.

As with [[Getting it Done#Full Contests|full contests]], the combatants take turns using their abilities. The other guy may swing first, but the fighter with the highest rating seizes the **initiative**. Unlike an ordinary contest, in a fight it is usually advantageous to strike first.

#### Surprise
Characters are surprised when they suddenly find themselves in a dangerous situation. Surprised characters go last in combat, and suffer a +2 increase to all General Ability Difficulties for any  immediate subsequent action. In a fight, this increase applies to the first round of combat.

Surprise NPCs by sneaking up on them with a successful Infiltration or Surveillance test. A sudden, surprising combat
move, such as an exploding bomb vest, or a seemingly helpless victim pulling a canister of pepper spray may require a different test: Explosives (to trigger it at the right psychological moment) or Disguise (to seem helpless until too late), for example. Usually “surprise” tests during a combat or confrontation begin at Difficulty 5; foes are just naturally more alert at such times. 

Agents avoid being surprised with a successful Sense Trouble test.

The Director may rule that very badly surprised characters (NPCs when the agents made their Infiltration test by 4 or more points; the agents, if they failed their Sense Trouble test by 4 or more points) may also forfeit one or more rounds of action. The +2 Difficulty penalty then applies to the first round in which they can act.

#### Initiative
The player character's rating determines initiative in combat. In a 2-way fight, Agents go first if:

> **In melee:**
> - Their Hand-to-Hand rating equals or exceeds their opponents.
> - They have a melee weapon already drawn and their Weapons rating equals or exceeds their opponent's  
>
> **In a gunfight:**
> - Their Shooting rating *exceeds* their opponents.
> 
> **In a mixed fight:**
> - They are wielding a gun.
> 
> *Otherwise, their opponent goes first.*

> [!tip] Larger Fights
> This works much the same way in fights with more than two combatant. In these cases, the Director abandons the aggressor/defender model. 
> 
> Instead, they determine an order of action, ranking all participants in the combat according to their ratings in the fighting skills they'll be starting the fight with (ties are broken with pools). 

> [!warning] **Remember:** Surprised combatants always go last!

#### Running Away
Fleeing from an ongoing melee requires an Athletics test. The Difficulty is 3 plus the number of foes you're fleeing from. On a success, melee ends and you flee; if they give chase, your foes must roll first in the contests. 

If you fail, the opponent with the highest damage value automatically deals one instance of damage to you. Melee still ends, but you roll first if a chase ensues. 

#### Hit Thresholds
Each character has a Hit Threshold. For Agents, this is 3 (the standard value), or 4 (if their Athletics rating is 8+). This Hit Threshold is the Difficulty the character's opponents must match or beat in order to harm them. 

Hit Thresholds for NPCs vary greatly: Less competent NPCs may have lower Hit Thresholds, while stronger opponents would typically parallel player character levels. Creatures both natural and supernatural may even have Hit Thresholds of 4 or higher, regardless of their Athletics ratings.

#### Dealing Damage
When you roll on or over your opponent's Hit Threshold, you may deal damage to him. To do so, you make a damage roll, rolling a die which is then modified according to the relative lethality of your weapon, based on the following table:

| Weapon Type                                                                                 | Damage Modifer |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Fist, kick                                                                                  | -2             |
| Blackjack, small improvised weapon, police baton, shuriken, stake, knife                    | -1             |
| Machete, large improvised weapon, light firearm (.32 pistol, 6.5mm rifle, 20-gauge shotgun) | +0             |
| Sword, fire axe, heavy firearm (9mm pistol, .30 rifle, 12-gauge shotgun)                    | +1             |
| Very heavy firearm (.50MG, 12.7x99mm anti-materiel rifle)                                   | +2             |
Characters may never spend points from their combat pools to increase their damage rolls. The final damage result is then subtracted from the opponent's Health pool. When a combatant's Health pool drops to 0 or less, that combatant collapses from exhaustion or blood loss and is unable to continue fighting. Any combatants currently engaged with him in melee can then deal another instance of damage to him.

> [!warning] **Tip:** Characters need reload only when dramatically appropriate. Otherwise, they're assumed to be able to refill the cylinders of their revolvers or swap magazines between shots! 

#### Exhaustion, Injury, and Death
Unlike most abilities, your Health pool can drop below 0. When this happens, you must make a Consciousness roll. Roll a die with the absolute value of your current Health pool as your Difficulty.

> [!warning] You may deliberately strain yourself to remain conscious, raising your die result by an amount of your choice. After the roll is resolved, your Health pool is reduced by the same amount.

*If your Health pool is between 0 and -5 inclusive*, you are **Hurt**, but have suffered no permanent injury beyond grazes, a few superficial cuts, and bruises, or "through-and-through" gunshot wounds. You must make a Consciousness roll. 

However, the pain of your injuries increases the Difficulty of all tests and contests, including opponents' Hit Thresholds, by 1. To spend points on Investigative abilities, you must make a Consciousness roll.

*If your Health pool is between -6 and -11 inclusive*, you have been **Seriously Wounded**. You must make a Consciousness roll.

Whether or not you maintain consciousness, you are no longer able to fight. Until you receive [[Combat#First Aid|first aid]], you will lose an additional Health point every half hour. 

Even after you receive first aid, you must convalesce in a hospital or similar setting for a period of days.

*When your Health pool dips to -12 or below*, you are **dead**. Time to create a replacement character.

#### First Aid
If you are wounded, a character with the Medic ability can improve your condition by spending Medic points. For every Medic point spent, you regain 2 Health points — unless you are your own Medic, in which case you gain only 1 Health point for every Medic point you spent. 

The Medic can only refill your pool to where you were before the incident in which you received this latest injury, and they must be in a position to devote all of their attention to directly tending to your wounds. 

Even with 0 points left in their Medic pool, a character with Medic can restore 1 Health point per scene to another character or themselves.

#### Standard Combat Considerations
This section describes numerous tweaks, modifiers and changes that can apply to standard combat.
##### Armour
If you're wearing a form of armour effective against the weapon being used against you, you subtract a number of points from each instance of damage dealt to you before applying it to your Health pool. 

Relatively concealable *ballistic cloth armour* can be worn under jackets or other loose clothing, reducing each instance of damage from bullets by 1 point. *Light body armour*, such as police tactical vests, reduces each instance of damage from bullets by 2 points, and from cutting and stabbing weapons by 1 point. *Military-grade body armour* reduces bullet or explosive damage by 3 points.

Light and military-grade body armour are heavy, hot, obvious and uncomfortable, increasing the Difficulty for most tests and attracting the attention of the locals. Even concealable armour may be visible to an enemy who knows what to look for. 

##### Cover
In a typical gunfight, combatants seek cover, hiding behind walls, furniture or other barriers, exposing themselves only for the few seconds it takes them to zero in on a target and fire. There are three cover conditions:
- **Exposed:** No barrier stands between you and the combatant firing at you. Your Hit Threshold decreases by 1.
- **Partial Cover:** About half of your body is exposed to fire. Your Hit Threshold remains unchanged. This is also the condition if you have full cover, but only behind thin or flimsy material like foliage, drywall, or canvas. 
- **Full Cover:** Except when you pop up to fire a round, the barrier completely protects you from incoming fire. Your Hit Threshold increases by 1.

##### Range
To keep Night's Black Agents games running smoothly, the ranges of firefights are abstracted into four broad groups:

> [!tip] **Point-Blank**  
> You are literally face-to-face with your opponent, within easy arm's reach. All brawls, knife fights, and scuffles occur at this range. 
> 
> At this range, all firearms do an additional +2 points of damage, and all shotguns are considered heavy firearms with a base damage modifier of +1.

> [!tip] **Close**  
> You are in the same room with your opponent, or within no more than 10 yards or so. A swordfight or a karate match might happen at this range, with plenty of bounding off or smashing through the furniture.
>   
>   At this range, all shotguns do an additional +1 point of damage. This is as far as you can throw an object directly at a target unless the object is specifically designed for throwing.

> [!tip] **Near**  
> You can see your opponent distinctly, perhaps across a warehouse or across the street, no more than 30 or 40 meters away. At this range, all shotguns are considered light firearms, with a base damage modifier of +0, and do no extra damage. This is the farthest range at which you can hit a target with a shotgun at all, or with a pistol or submachine gun. This is as far as you can throw any object. 
>   
>   Thrown weapons at Near range do -2 damage, and the target's Hit Threshold is 1 higher, unless you have the [[General Abilities List#Weapons|Quincey Morris' Bowie Knife]] cherry.

> [!tip] **Long**  
> Your opponent is within 100 yards. Opponents farther away cannot be reliably targeted at all, and are out of the combat. This is the farthest range at which you can hit a target with a rifle or assault rifle.

> [!warning] **Extending Ranges**  
> You can spend 2 points from your Shooting pool to attempt to hit a target at Long range with a pistol or submachine gun, or at up to 500 yards with a rifle or assault rifle. If the rifle is specially designed as a sniper rifle, with a bipod and a scope, the range extends up to 1,000 yards. 
>   
>  This spend does not adjust your roll; it makes it possible in the first place.


##### Special Weapons Training
If you have a Weapons or Shooting rating of 8+, you can spend 6 General Ability build points to grant yourself Special Weapons Training. 

If your Weapons rating is 8+, choose a particular make of weapon: for example, a switchblade, a tonfa, a stiletto, a garrotte.

If your Shooting rating is 8+, choose a particular make of firearm: for example, a Walther PPK, Heckler & Koch MP5 submachine gun, .357 Desert Eagle. 

In your hands, your chosen weapon increases its damage by 1. This applies not only to the particular weapon you own, but to all other weapons of the exact make.
