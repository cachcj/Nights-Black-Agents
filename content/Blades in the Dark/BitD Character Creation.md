### Before You Begin: A Crew of Scoundrels
Character creation and crew creation are typically intertwined. The characters you play may inform the crew you wish to form, or the nature of the crew may inform the type of character you wish to play.


### Part One: The Scoundrels
Every player character in *Blades in the Dark* is a daring criminal of Doskvol, known in common parlance as a scoundrel. They are familiar with all of the various feats of skulduggery represented by the **actions** of the game. They can **SKIRMISH** in a knife-fight, **PROWL** in the shadows, **ATTUNE** to strange energy, **CONSORT** with contacts for information, and so on.

Because of the way the dice system works, every scoundrel can roll a 2d for any action in the game. Pretty good, statistically — a 75% chance of success. This means that even "zero rating" actions on your sheet don't represent deficiencies or gaps of skill; they indicate the actions for which your character is likely to burn stress and rely on teamwork. 

#### Step 1 — Choose a Playbook
 
A **playbook** is your character type. It determines your starting action ratings, your special abilities, and your XP triggers. It's also how other scoundrels in the underworld know you — these are street names, not job titles. A playbook is a starting point, not a cage; it represents your area of focus, not your immutable nature.

A **playbook** is what we call the sheet with all the specific rules to play a certain character type in *Blades in the Dark*. By choosing a playbook, you're choosing which type of scoundrel your character is — they **special abilities** (with which they break the rules in various ways) they possess, and their **xp triggers** (how they earn experience for character advancement). 

The names of the playbooks function are underworld slang in Duskwall. These are:

> Each playbook has 6–8 special abilities to choose from at creation (and unlock through advancement). Choose **one** to start. If you can't decide, the first ability listed on your sheet is placed there as a good default.

> Some special abilities refer to your **special armour**. Each character sheet has a set of three boxes to track usage of armour (standard, heavy, and special). If you have any abilities that uses your special armour, tick its box when you activate one of them. If you don't have any special abilities that use special armour, then you can't use that armour box at all.

#### Step 2 — Choose a Heritage
 
Your **heritage** describes where your family line is from. You may be of any heritage of your choice, and then decide if you're a foreigner who has immigrated to Doskvol or if you're a local who grew up here. Write a detail about your family life. For example, you might choose **Skovlan** heritage, and then write *Factory workers from Lockport, now refugees.*

#### Step 3 — Choose a Background
 
Your character's **background** describes what they did before they joined the crew. Choose one, then write a detail about it that's specific to your character. For example, you could choose **Labor**, and then write *Leviathan hunter sailor, mutineer.*
 
- **Academic** — A scholar, a professor or student from Doskvol Academy, a philosopher or journalist, etc.
- **Labor** — A servant, a factory worker, a coach driver, a docker, a sailor, etc. *A stevedore for the North Hook Company.*
- **Law** — An advocate or barrister, a Bluecoat or inspector (or even Spirit Warden), a prison guard from Ironhook, a Rail Jack, etc.
- **Military** — An Imperial or Skovlander soldier, a mercenary, an intelligence operative, a strategist, a training instructor, etc.
- **Noble** — A dilettante, a courtier, the scion of a fallen house, etc
- **Trade** — A shopkeeper, a merchant, a skilled crafts-person, a shipping agent, etc. *A liaison to the Ministry of Preservation.*
- **Underworld** — A street urchin, gang member, young thug, or other outcast who grew up on the streets.

 ---
#### Step 4 — Assign Four Action Dots
 
Your playbook begins with **three action dots** already placed. You get to add **four more**, for a total of seven. At the start of the game, no action may start above **rating 2** (unless a Special Ability tells you otherwise).
 
Assign them as follows:
1. One dot in any action that reflects your **heritage**.
2. One dot in any action that reflects your **background**.
3. Two dots **anywhere you choose** (max rating is 2, remember).

##### [[BitD Actions|The 12 Actions]]
 
Actions are grouped into three **attributes** — Insight, Prowess, and Resolve. Your attribute rating is the number of dots in its actions, used for resistance rolls (see Basic Mechanics).
 
**Insight** *(mental acuity, knowledge, attention)*
 
| Action      | Description                                              |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Attune**  | Open your mind to the ghost field or other arcane power. |
| **Command** | Compel swift obedience.                                  |
| **Study**   | Scrutinise details and interpret evidence.               |
| **Survey**  | Observe a situation and anticipate outcomes.             |
 
**Prowess** *(physical capability, agility, force)*
 
| Action       | What it covers                                                 |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Finesse**  | Employ dextrous manipulation or subtle misdirection.           |
| **Hunt**     | Carefully track a target.                                      |
| **Prowl**    | Traverse skillfully and quietly.                               |
| **Skirmish** | Entangle a target in close combat so they can't easily escape. |
 
**Resolve** *(mental fortitude, willpower, presence)*
 
| Action      | What it covers                            |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Consort** | Socialise with friends and contacts.      |
| **Sway**    | Influence with guile, charm, or argument. |
| **Tinker**  | Fiddle with devices and mechanisms.       |
| **Wreck**   | Unleash savage force..                    |
 
> [!note] **Many actions overlap.**  
> This is intentional. You choose which action your character is *performing* — then the GM sets position and effect for that choice. "Wreck someone in a fight" is possible; it might just be riskier or less effective than Skirmish. The player chooses the action; the GM frames the consequences.

#### Step 5 — Choose a Special Ability
 
Take a look at the special abilities for your playbook and choose one. If you can't decide which one to pick, go with the first one on the list—it's placed there as a good default choice.

> [!note] **Special Armour**  
> Some special abilities refer to your **special armour**. Each character sheet has a set of three boxes to track usage of armour (standard, heavy, and special). If you have any abilities that uses your special armour, tick its box when you activate one of them. If you don't have any special abilities that use special armour, then you can't use that armour box at all.

#### Step 6 — Choose One Friend and One Rival
 
Each playbook has a list of potential NPC friends. Choose one from the list who is a close relationship (a good friend, a lover, a family relation, etc.). Mark the upwardpointing triangle next to their name. Then choose another NPC on the list who's your rival or enemy. Mark the downward-pointing triangle next to their name.

#### Step 7 — Choose Your Vice
 
Every scoundrel is in thrall to some **vice** or another, which they indulge to deal with stress. Choose a vice from the list, and describe it on the line above with the specific details and the name and location of your **vice purveyor** in the city. For example, you might choose **Gambling**, then write *Spogg's dice game, Crow's Foot*. You can create a vice purveyor or ask the GM to tell you about a couple likely options for your vice.

See a list of vice purveyors [[BitD Duskwall: Vice Purveyors|here]].
 
| Vice           | What it looks like                                                                                            |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Faith**      | You’re dedicated to an unseen power, forgotten god, ancestor, etc.                                            |
| **Gambling**   | You crave games of chance, betting on sporting events, etc.                                                   |
| **Luxury**     | Expensive or ostentatious displays of opulence.                                                               |
| **Obligation** | You’re devoted to a family, a cause, an organization, a charity, etc.                                         |
| **Pleasure**   | Gratification from lovers, food, drink, drugs, art, theatre, etc.                                             |
| **Stupor**     | You seek oblivion in the abuse of drugs, drink to excess, getting beaten to a pulp in the fighting pits, etc. |
| **Weird**      | You experiment with strange essences, consort with rogue spirits, observe bizarre rituals or taboos, etc.     |
 
You **must** indulge your vice during downtime or you take stress equal to your trauma. If you *overindulge* (clear more stress than you had), something goes wrong — you attract trouble, brag about your crimes, disappear for weeks, or get cut off by your purveyor.
 
Playing to your vice also earns you XP at end of session.
 
---
 
#### Step 8 — Record Name, Alias, and Look
 
Choose or invent a name. Note an alias or street nickname if your character uses one. Write a few evocative words describing their appearance. Sample names and look descriptors are on your playbook sheet.
 
---
 
#### Review Your Details
Take a look at the details on your character sheet, especially the **experience triggers** for your playbook (like “Earn xp when you address a challenge with knowledge or arcane power,” for example) and the **special items** available to a character of your type (like the Whisper's spirit mask, for example). You begin with access to all of the items on your sheet, so don't worry about picking specific things—you'll decide what your character is carrying later on, when you're on the job (see **[[BitD Loadout|Loadout]]**). 

That's it! Your character is ready for play. When you start the first session, the GM will ask you some questions about who you are, your outlook, or some past events. If you don't know the answers, make some up. Or ask the other players for ideas

#### Loadout (Decided Per Score, Not at Creation)
 
You have access to **all items listed on your playbook sheet** — you don't choose them now. Before each score, you decide your **load** for that operation, and check off which items you're bringing, up to that number.
 
| Load | Category | What it signals |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | **Light** | Faster, less conspicuous; you blend in with citizens |
| 4–5 | **Normal** | You look like a scoundrel ready for trouble |
| 6+ | **Heavy** | You're slower; you look like an operative on a mission |
 
One unit of coin in silver pieces takes up one item slot. Some items take two slots (shown with two connected boxes on your sheet). Items in *italics* don't count toward load.

### Part Two: The Crew
 
Regardless of how a crew comes to be formed, they all have one thing in common: they exist to create a legacy that will last beyond the founding members. When you start a crew with your partners, you intend to build something that (hopefully) will live on past the scope of your own criminal careers. This is why we have a separate character sheet for the crew — tracking its development, growth, and influence.

In a sense, the crew is the central figure in the stories we're going to tell about the underworld of Doskvol. Scoundrels come and go — burned out due to **Trauma**, or killed, or forever lost to their vices, or, if they're very lucky, gone to some comfortable retirement — but the crew carries on. New blood comes in, new characters with new outlooks and drives, and new stories to be told.
 
#### Step 1 — Choose a Crew Type
 
Your crew type determines the scores that you'll focus on, as well as a selection of special abilities that support that kind of action. The crew type isn't meant to be restrictive — a crew of Smugglers might sometimes engage in extortion (like Bravos) or sell contraband (like Hawkers) — but the core activity of the crew type is the most frequent way they earn **coin** and xp for advancement. 

There are six different types of crew to choose from.
 
| Crew Type     | Description                                                                                                        |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Assassins** | Killers for hire. They execute “accidents,” disappearances, murders, and ransoms.                                  |
| **Bravos**    | Mercenaries and thugs. They execute battles, extortion, sabotage, and smash & grabs.                               |
| **Cult**      | Acolytes of a forgotten god. They execute artifact acquisitions, auguries, consecration, and sacrifices.           |
| **Hawkers**   | Vice dealers. They execute product procurement, covert sales, shows of force, and social events.                   |
| **Shadows**   | Thieves and spies. They execute burglaries, espionage, robberies, and sabotage.                                    |
| **Smugglers** | Contraband transporters. They execute clandestine deliveries, territory control, and expeditions outside the city. |
 
> Like a character playbook, your crew type is also how you're known in the underworld of Doskvol. The criminal factions and the Bluecoats think of you as “assassins” or “a cult” etc., and will treat you accordingly.

Each crew begins with **2 coin** in its coffers (the remains of the PCs savings). You are **Tier 0**, with **weak hold** and **0 rep**.

---
 
#### Step 2 — Choose a Reputation and Lair

Your crew has just formed and acquired a lair. Given this group of characters and their previous escapades, what initial reputation would you have among the factions of the underworld? Choose one from this list(or create your own):

> **Ambitious**, **Brutal**, **Daring**, **Honourable**, **Professional**, **Savvy**, **Subtle**, **Strange**.

You earn xp when you bolster your crew's reputation, so think of this as another cue to indicate what sorts of action you want in the game. Will you be recklessly ambitious, targeting higher-Tier targets? Will you take on daring scores that others deem too risky? Are you interested in the strange weirdness of the Dusk?

Also, talk about where the crew makes its **lair**. You begin at Tier 0, so it's probably a very modest or abandoned sort of place. Choose one (or create your own):

> - A **half-sunken grotto** in the city's maze-like underground canals.
> - An **abandoned watch tower** atop an ancient, crumbling wall.
> - The **unassuming back rooms** of a merchant's shop.
> - A **small, abandoned house** at the end of a dark lane.
> - A **rickety, tin-roofed shack** perched on a rooftop.
> - A **junked rail-car**, rusted in place on its old, overgrown tracks.

Look at the [[BitD Duskwall Map|map of Duskwall]] and choose the district in which your lair is found. Are you based in some derelict area of a wealthy district or is your lair one of many other poor hovels in a destitute area? 

 
---
 
#### Step 3 — Establish Hunting Grounds

Your crew is brand-new, but you have chosen some small part of a district as your **hunting grounds**. This is the area that you usually target for your scores, and you know it well. Your hunting grounds don't have to be in the same district as your lair. The area is small, only three or four city blocks—but it's still an intrusion on someone. The entire city is divided among larger, stronger factions. The GM will tell you which faction claims the area, then you decide how to deal with them: 

- Pay them off. Give them **1 coin** in exchange for giving you room to work. 
- Pay the faction **2 coin** as a show of respect and gain **+1 status** with them. 
- Keep your money and take **-1 status** with that faction.

Your hunting grounds are useful for a particular type of criminal operation. Each crew type has a list of different operation types for their hunting grounds. Choose one of these operation types as your preference.

When you prepare to execute an operation of your preferred type on your hunting grounds, you get **+1d** to any **gather information** rolls and a free additional **downtime activity** to contribute to that operation. This can help you discover an opportunity, acquire an asset you might need for the job, find an appropriate client, etc.

---

#### Step 4 — Choose a Crew Special Ability
 
Take a look at the special abilities for your crew and choose one. If you can't decide which one to pick, go with the first one on the list—it's placed there as a good default choice. It's important to pick a special ability that everyone is excited about. You can get more special abilities in the future by earning xp. 

Just like picking the crew type, reputation, lair, and hunting grounds, choosing a special ability is another chance to focus the game down to a more specific range of possibilities. 


---
 
#### Step 5 — Assign Crew Upgrades

An upgrade is a valuable asset that helps the crew in some way, like a boat or a gang (see [[BitD Crew Upgrades|here]] for the complete descriptions!). Each crew type has two pre-selected **upgrades** that suit that crew.

You get to add **two additional upgrades** to your new crew (so you'll have a total of four upgrades when you start). You can choose from the specific upgrades available to your crew type or the general upgrades on the crew sheet.

When you assign your two upgrades, the GM will tell you about two factions that are impacted by your choices: 
- One faction helped you get an upgrade. They like you, and you get **+1 status** with them. At your option, spend **1 coin** to repay their kindness, and take **+2 status** with them instead. 
- One faction was screwed over when you got an upgrade. They don't like you, and you get **-2 status** with them. At your option, spend **1 coin** to mollify them, and take **-1 status** with them instead. 

You'll be able get more upgrades in the future by earning [[BitD Advancement|xp]].

---
 
#### Step 6 — Choose a Favourite Contact
 
Your crew sheet lists several potential contacts. Choose one as a close ally or partner. The GM then tells you about two factions that are impacted by your choice:
- One faction is **friendly** with this contact, and you get **+1 status** with them
- One faction is **unfriendly** with this contact, and you get **-1 status** with them
At your option, these factions are even more concerned with this contact and so you take +2 and -2 status instead.

## Advancement
 
### Character XP
 
Mark XP on your character sheet during and after each session:
 
- **During the session:** Mark 1 XP in an attribute when you make a **desperate action roll** using one of its actions.
- **End of session:** Review your XP triggers. Mark 1 XP (or 2 if it happened a lot) for each:
  - Your **playbook-specific trigger** (listed on your sheet — e.g. the Cutter's is "Address a challenge with violence or coercion")
  - You **expressed your beliefs, drives, heritage, or background**
  - You **struggled with your vice or traumas** (overindulging counts; simply indulging doesn't)
You can apply end-of-session XP to any track you choose.
 
When a **playbook XP track** fills, take a new special ability. When an **attribute XP track** fills, add one action dot to any action under that attribute (max rating 3; max 4 with the crew's Mastery upgrade).
 
### Crew XP
 
At end of session, review the crew XP triggers and mark 1 (or 2 if significant) for each:
- Your **crew-specific trigger** (e.g. Smugglers: "Execute a smuggling operation or acquire new clients or contraband sources")
- You **contended with challenges above your current station**
- You **bolstered your reputation or developed a new one**
- You **expressed the goals, drives, inner conflict, or essential nature of the crew**
When the crew XP track fills, take a new crew special ability, or mark two upgrade boxes.
 
Every time the crew advances, each PC also receives **+1 stash** (+2 per crew Tier) as profit from ongoing operations.
