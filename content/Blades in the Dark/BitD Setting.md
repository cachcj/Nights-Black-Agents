### Doskvol

The city of **Doskvol** — also known as *Duskwall* in Akorosian slang, or *North Hook* in Imperial nautical designation, or simply  *"the Dusk"* — was established over 1000 years ago as a coal mining settlement on the cold north coast of **Akoros**. It has withstood the breaking of the world, an attack by a titanic leviathan, massive fires, a plague, a civil war, and legions of angry ghosts. It is a community of survivors.

The city is densely packed inside the ring of immense lightning towers that protect it from the murderous ghosts of the blighted **deathlands** beyond. Every square foot is covered in human construction of some kind—piled one atop another with looming towers, sprawling manors, and stacked row houses; dissected by canals and narrow twisting alleys; connected by a spiderweb of roads, bridges, and elevated walkways.

Doskvol is one of the most important cities in the Imperium, since it is from its port that the metal steamships of the leviathan hunters are launched. The leviathan hunters sail out in their gargantuan metal ships, braving the far northern reaches of the Void Sea, far out of sight of land, to grapple with titanic demons of the depths and extract their precious immortal blood—the substance refined into **electroplasm**, the power source of civilization.

All powerful noble families operate hunter ships, each commanded by the scion of their line—and it is by their fortunes at sea and the bounties of blood they capture that the fortunes of the empire wax and wane. The savvy and the ruthless of Doskvol do well to position themselves to profit from this crucial enterprise upon which so many depend—either as an ally or servant of the aristocracy, or by preying upon the corrupted rich and privileged elite.

Like the other cities of the empire, Doskvol is encircled by crackling lightning towers. These walls of lightning keep out the vengeful spirits and twisted horrors of the deathlands *out*, while keeping all its citizens *in*. The city is a pressure cooker, and you are inside it.
 
### The People of Doskvol
 
Doskvol is a cosmopolitan and fractious city. Three main cultural groups make up most of the population:
 
**Akorosi** are the most common, claiming the same heritage as the Immortal Emperor himself. They form the bulk of the nobility and ruling class.
 
**Skovlanders** are the original indigenous people of the north. Many Skovlander refugees have come to Doskvol throughout the *Unity War*, building up a large population of laborers, crafts-people, and workers. Some Akorosi regard them as traitors to the Empire and treat them poorly.
 
**Iruvians** are a powerful and wealthy dominion in the south, claiming nobility via an ancient pact with the Emperor. They maintain an influential consulate in Duskwall and keep a close eye on their portion of the leviathan hunter fleet.
 
The three common languages are **Akorosian** (the Imperial common tongue — verbose and nuanced), **Skovic** (the Skovlander mother tongue — clipped and direct), and **Hadrathi** (an ancient tongue, mostly found in old texts and among some Iruvians, prized for its elaborate expletives).


### Lights in the Darkness

Days are divided into two parts, marked by the weak lights at dawn and twilight. The 12 hours after dawn are numbered; the first hour, the second hour, the third hour, etc. The 12 hours after twilight are named, usually according to local custom, which varies from city to city. During certain phases of its transit, the moon appears to multiply across the sky, in pairs and trios of sibling lights, as if reflected on the facets of a vast crystalline dome. It is not known what causes these pale, dimmer sisters to appear, but occultists and natural philosophers have no shortage of arcane explanations and scientific hypotheses. 

The ancient stars still hang in the black sky, though their arrangements sometimes swirl and change according to unknown principles of celestial motion. When the oceans turned to black ink during the cataclysm, a new realm of constellations appeared in the depths of the seas—millions of tiny pinpoints of light, somehow clearly visible far below the waves.

Doskvol, like all the great cities of the Imperium, operates massive generators around the clock, to produce the power needed for **lightning barriers** as well as the thousands of electroplasmic lights that illuminate the public thoroughfares. Wealthy citizens operate their own generators and electric lights, but most people make do with simple candles and lanterns in their homes and shops.

Because of the perpetual darkness, a small hand lantern is practically a required tool for daily life in the city. Common people use simple oil lanterns, while the more well-to-do employ the most advanced miniature electroplasmic lamps, clipped to a belt to keep their hands free, mounted on the head of a walking stick, or carried by a servant lamp-bearer. 

Areas of illicit dealings or ill-repute sometimes enforce a “no lamps” policy, so business may be conducted in the anonymous darkness.

### Electroplasm

The lifeblood of the empire — literally. **Electroplasm** (or just *plasm*) is refined from leviathan blood: a thick, black, iridescent, oily substance harvested from the monsters of the deep. The refining process is toxic, volatile, and kept as a close trade secret by the noble houses that control it.
 
Refined electroplasm powers everything: the lightning barriers, the electric street lamps, the rail lines that connect the cities of the Imperium. It also powers the **ghost field** — not merely the energy contained within the lightning barrier of the city, but the echoes of events in the recent past, and the medium in which spirits exist.
 
The common slang term is *plasm*. When things go wrong with electroplasm — and they do — the consequences tend to involve ghosts, spiritual contamination, or explosions. Sometimes all three.

### The Ghost Field & The Haunted City
 
When the Gates of Death shattered, the dead stopped passing on. Instead, they linger — drawn to life, craving what they've lost. The empire's response has been the **Spirit Wardens**, an organisation empowered by the Immortal Emperor to hunt down rogue spirits and destroy them in electroplasmic crematorium fires. They operate independently of local law enforcement.
 
Despite the Wardens' efforts, ghosts are a fact of daily life in Doskvol. Every citizen has had at least one harrowing encounter with a rogue spirit. Most clutch **spiritbane charms** and know a half-remembered invocation or two. When things get serious, they hire a **Whisper**.


The kinds of spirits you may encounter:
 
- **Echoes** — loops of behaviour captured in the ghost field, repeating endlessly. No will of their own, but still dangerous due to the electrical discharge of their plasmic forms. Sometimes they spontaneously manifest at the site of extreme violence, even without death.
- **Specters** — malevolent spirits seeking to possess a victim, drain its life essence, or take vengeance on those they believe wronged them in life.
- **Horrors** — non-human electroplasmic entities native to the ghost field. Rare inside the city, common in the deathlands.
- **Reconciled** — ghosts that have somehow remained sane and do not crave life or vengeance. Extremely rare. Some spectrologists don't believe they exist.
- **Spirit Wells** — rifts in reality where ghosts and supernatural beings congregate to draw energy. In ancient myth, spawning grounds for demons.
The ghost field can be attuned to, manipulated, and exploited — but doing so is rarely safe or simple.

### Law & Order
 
Doskvol's law enforcement is layered, corrupt, and thoroughly class-stratified. Here is what matters for a scoundrel:
 
**The City Watch — "Bluecoats"** — patrol the streets, make arrests, and run Ironhook Prison. They are theoretically law enforcement. In practice, they serve the interests of whoever pays them, harassing the poor and protecting the powerful. They operate like another street gang with legal cover.
 
**The Inspectors — "Constables"** — investigate crimes and build cases. Unlike the Bluecoats, they are famously incorruptible — often foreigners specifically appointed because they have no local ties and can't be bought. They are the real threat when a crime is taken seriously at the institutional level.
 
**The Magistrates** — issue warrants, prosecute trials, and pronounce verdicts. There are no juries. Justice is commensurate with wealth and political connection.
 
**The Spirit Wardens** — not technically city law enforcement; they answer directly to the Immortal Emperor. Their mandate is the destruction of rogue spirits. They operate Bellweather Crematorium, where all recovered corpses are dissolved in electroplasmic fire to prevent them rising as ghosts. If you have anything to do with spirits, the Wardens eventually become relevant.
 
The city's unofficial operating principle: *everyone is guilty in Duskwall, so make sure you get rich before you get caught.*

### The Underworld
 
The powerful elite of the city work to keep the lower classes in want and servitude. Some of those at the bottom find their way out through crime — the only tools available to them: skulduggery, violence, and the dark arts.
 
The underworld factions that survive long enough often become what they once fought against. Power corrupts, and ascension to influence usually means becoming another agent of control. The cycle perpetuates: violent resistance, violent response, and either violent ends or ascension to the overlord's perch.
 
At the apex of the underworld sit two organisations that have already made that climb:
 
**The Unseen** — a secretive criminal organisation whose members are said to be placed within every institution in Doskvol. They profit from vice and extortion at all levels of society.
 
**The Hive** — an Imperially-recognised "merchant guild" with many legitimate businesses, covering a much more profitable trade in contraband and human trafficking.
 
Below them, the rest of the underworld is in constant war — vicious competition among gangs and crews, each scrambling to survive and grow. The landscape of criminal power shifts constantly: street to street, block to block, district to district. Power is never stable. Alliances are fleeting. Vendettas are bloody.
 
Your crew starts at the bottom of this ladder. The question is what you're willing to do to climb it — and who you'll become by the time you do.

Foolhardy expeditions occasionally venture into both.

### Tone & Touchstones
 
Blades in the Dark plays out like a dark crime drama with gothic horror edges. The tone is gritty and grounded: these are desperate people doing dangerous things in a dangerous world. There are victories, but they come at cost. Every action has consequences.
 
**What the game feels like:**
 
- Criminal ambition and its price
- The corruption of power, even hard-won power
- Gothic atmosphere: fog, gaslight, ghosts, and rain
- Tight, pressure-cooker urban intrigue with no exit
- Vices, loyalties, and the things that make people human even in the dark

**Reference points** (pick your flavour):
 
- *Peaky Blinders* — criminal enterprise, gang politics, personal vice, the cost of ambition
- *The Lies of Locke Lamora* — elaborate heists, found family, dark city, dangerous people
- *Dishonored* — industrial fantasy, supernatural intrigue, assassination and consequence
- *Bloodborne* — gothic horror, eldritch forces, the weight of what lurks below the surface
- *The Wire* — institutional corruption, the ecosystem of crime, no one gets out clean
